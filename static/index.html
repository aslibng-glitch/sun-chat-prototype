LOCAL COMMUNITIES

You're not alone. Connect with local communities for Turkish women

Local Community Centres and Organisations

Day-Mer (Turkish and Kurdish Community Centre)
Established in London in 1989, Day-Mer is a long-standing community centre that supports women's integration into society, access to social rights, and efforts to reduce social isolation. It has a dedicated women's section and runs programmes specifically for women.
IMECE Women's Centre
Provides support to Turkish, Kurdish, Turkish Cypriot and other migrant women experiencing issues such as violence, discrimination and poverty. It offers guidance, counselling and advocacy services.
TWPA (Turkish Women's Philanthropic Association)
One of the UK's long-established Turkish women's organisations, TWPA focuses on education, philanthropy and strengthening community connections.
IKWRO (Women's Rights Organisation)
Provides vital support and advice to migrant women, including Turkish-language support, particularly around issues such as so-called honour-based abuse, domestic violence and forced marriage.

Local and Online Networks

Community & Social Groups
Informal women's communities, such as Rise Her Collective, organise social meet-ups, outings and other activities, helping women build connections and support networks.
Social Media Communities
Facebook groups such as Göçmen Kadınlar provide spaces where migrant women can exchange information, connect with one another, find peer support and navigate life in the UK.

Safety Tips

Here are some tips for keeping you and your loved ones safe online.

One thing worth knowing: changing a setting, for example changing a password, can sometimes let the other person know something has changed, and that can make their reaction worse. So when you make a change can matter as much as what you change. If you are not sure, it can be safer to talk it through with someone you trust, or with the helpline below, before you make a change.

If you are in immediate danger, call 999. If you have concerns but it is not an emergency, you can call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline any time of day: 0808 2000 247.

1. Add a lock to your devices
Making sure your phone, laptop and tablet each have a lock, a PIN, fingerprint, or face ID, is a good first step. When picking a PIN, it helps to avoid anything easy to guess, like your birthday, part of your name, or a simple sequence like "1234", and to avoid reusing the same code across more than one device. You can usually set or change this in a few taps under your device's "Settings" menu.
2. Keep an eye on your browsing history
The sites you visit and when you visited them are usually saved as a history in your browser. If you're worried someone else might see this, you have a few options: use your browser's "private" mode (which doesn't save history), clear your history by hand after each use, or check the separate records kept by search engines like Google or Bing. Women's Aid's Cover Your Tracks Online page walks through this step by step for each browser.
3. Set your devices to lock themselves automatically
Having your phone or laptop lock on its own shortly after you put it down makes it harder for anyone to look through it without you knowing. This is usually found under a "Screen" or "Lock" setting.
4. Turn on two factor authentication
Adding a second layer beyond your password, like a code sent to your phone when you log in, means an account stays yours even if the password alone becomes known to someone else. It's worth turning this on separately for email, social media, and banking accounts.
5. Review who has access to shared accounts or cloud storage
If you use a shared account, Google Drive, iCloud, OneDrive, and similar, it's a good habit to check every so often who still has access. If there's someone you no longer want to share with, you can remove their access.
6. Refresh your social media privacy settings
Check who can see your profile, your friends or followers list, and what you post. You can block or unfollow people, or hide your friends list from others. Women's Aid's Cover Your Tracks Online page has current, platform by platform guides for Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, TikTok, and more.
7. Check your cloud backup settings
It's worth knowing whether your photos and files are being backed up automatically to a cloud service like iCloud or Google Photos, since that affects where your content is stored. You can turn this on or off at any time in your settings.
8. Change your PINs and passwords every so often
Refreshing your passwords from time to time, especially any that someone else may have known at some point, helps you take back control. Aim for a different, hard to guess password for each account.
